# Editorial audit: why the v2 revision is different

**Version:** v2.0.0-draft.1 · **Date:** 2026-11-19  
**Scope:** literary/philosophical edition plus a **separate** mathematical and empirical research programme. No external reviewer has endorsed this edition.

## Verdict before editing

The v1 treatise was coherent and readable, but too many paragraphs independently repeated familiar cautions: certainty is not accuracy, silence is ambiguous, liking is not willingness, experience changes us. These are valuable *premises*, not sufficient grounds for a distinctive philosophical theory. The original logical notation `H(A,B,t) ⇒ W_A(B,t) ∧ W_B(A,t)` expresses a condition already included in a voluntary dyadic relationship's definition; this is not a discovered law. The accompanying Lean 4 lemmas are formally meaningful only **relative to the exact toy definitions and premises**. Neither mathematical typography nor the Wittgenstein-inspired numbering should make ordinary observations look automatically profound.

The v1 text also gave its most promising distinction—**not yet known** versus **not yet formed**—near the end. Earlier chapters were therefore liable to read like an anthology of psychological observations rather than steps toward a main problem.

The revision **does not attempt to make the work 'great' by declaring it original**. It tries to make the chain of reasoning clearer and specify how the work could earn a significant contribution.

## The revised philosophical argument

1. **The object of knowledge (Chapter 1):** People are not identical to an observer's representation. A representation can nevertheless be accurate for a defined purpose. This rules out the facile view that first impressions are necessarily wrong.
2. **A decision-relative standard (Chapter 2):** A person need not know everything before choosing a specific next step. We must distinguish observed actions, explicitly communicated choices, and uncertain hidden motives. This rules out the equally facile view that every ambiguity must be investigated.
3. **Endogenous values (Chapter 3):** A person's stated ideals are not identical to actual evaluations; later reports need not identify whether a preference was discovered, formed, or reported differently. Thus the chooser is also a potential object of change.
4. **Endogenous relational state (Chapter 4):** Encounters may reveal properties, modify values, and create dyadic states. They may also have no such effects. These are conceptually separable but not automatically distinguishable from a few reports.
5. **Independent agency (Chapter 5):** There are two decision makers, not one researcher or optimizer controlling an outcome. Mutual voluntariness is constitutive; one person's epistemic improvement cannot force another's choice.
6. **Unrealized paths (Chapter 6):** Actual history can underdetermine counterfactual outcomes. A hypothetical better encounter is not evidence of a missed relationship; the very process could have altered both participants.
7. **Synthesis (Chapter 7):** Knowledge is partial and decision-relative; some relevant states develop jointly; not all possible developments should or need to be undertaken.

### What the argument does *not* show

- That persons are forever unknowable, or no words can express experience.
- That first impressions are always bad and more interaction always improves them.
- That interaction **necessarily** creates compatibility or attraction.
- That mutual willingness alone is sufficient for a feasible relationship.
- That uncertainty about feelings overrides someone's present decision.
- That an apparently emergent relationship effect proves causal constitution.
- That a small Lean 4 proof validates the manuscript's psychological claims.

## The strongest objection, stated fairly

**Objection:** The distinction between learning something and making something happen already occurs in dynamic epistemic logic; preference transformation is prominent in L. A. Paul's work; relationship-specific effects and shared experiences are well studied. Therefore the conceptual core is not new.

**Response:** Largely correct. The project must **not** advertise those components as unprecedented. Its best candidate for contribution is a tightly connected, two-agent decision problem which asks when distinct processes can be **told apart** and when each person's decision is sufficiently informed **without requiring complete identification**. Even this combination has neighboring work in interactive POMDPs, dual control, longitudinal causal inference, and relationship science. Its novelty remains **unestablished**, not assumed.

**More difficult objection:** A supposed 'relationship created by interaction' may merely be an unobserved stable compatibility parameter expressing itself after interaction.

**Response:** Also correct in general. An arbitrary stable latent response schedule can imitate dynamic outcomes; without restrictive measurement and causal assumptions, it is not possible to choose one account on the basis of an evocative narrative. The companion programme therefore proposes **restricted rivals** and a design which could falsify *those rivals*, while openly admitting alternative pathways.

**Ethical objection:** Investigating uncertain attraction can become rationalization for ignoring a refusal.

**Response:** The philosophical question “What were their hidden reasons?” and the practical question “May this relationship proceed?” have different targets. Their answers need not wait for one another. The new §§2.21, 4.51, and 5.32 are included to prevent the uncertainty thesis from becoming a warrant for pressure.

## Evidence ledger: scope of observed research

- **1992 Ambady & Rosenthal meta-analysis** and **1995 Funder accuracy model** counter overly skeptical claims about first impressions; neither validates an individual's particular inference.
- **2010 Vazire SOKA** distinguishes targets with different information access; supports making accuracy specific rather than universal.
- **1998 Laurenceau et al.** establish empirical precedent for disclosure/responsiveness and experienced intimacy. Feeling understood must not be conflated with independently checked accuracy.
- **2017 Joel et al.** show predictive failure of *predate trait/preference features* for *relationship-specific desire* in their studies. The finding is not a proof of impossibility.
- **2022 Baxter et al.** show initial dyad-specific impressions can predict later outcomes. This constrains claims that acquaintance must always start from nothing.
- **2014 L. A. Paul** and **2006 van Ditmarsch & Kooi** are direct philosophical and mathematical predecessors for transformative choice and epistemic/ontic changes.
- **29 October 2026 Eastwick, Zhao & Zietsch** is an exceptionally close recent conceptual predecessor for shared experiences and compatibility; treat it as a challenge that raises the novelty threshold.

Bibliographic links, precise interpretation, and proposed tests are recorded in [RESEARCH_PROGRAM.md](./RESEARCH_PROGRAM.md). This audit is not a systematic review and does not invent a literature-wide consensus.

## Standards for the next draft

The next draft should be **willing to fail**. Commission independent criticism; search for arguments already made by others; state where the model makes different predictions; operationalize a relational outcome with measurement invariance; and specify what would count as evidence against the preferred account. Reconcile philosophical wording with paper/Lean separately, leaving the literary source independent. Translate only after the Indonesian revision stabilizes.

**Bottom line:** v2 is a stronger *candidate philosophical treatise*. It is not yet a major scientific contribution. Its path toward significance lies in precise discriminations that survive criticism, not additional symbolic ornament.
