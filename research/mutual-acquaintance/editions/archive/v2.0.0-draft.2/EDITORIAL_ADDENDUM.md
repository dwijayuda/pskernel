| --- | --- | --- |
| **1.13** | Some understanding of another person's meaning is experienced directly through expression and action, not necessarily through explicit private inference | The original representational framing risks treating every instance of social understanding as hidden-state inference; direct perception and second-person accounts are serious competitors |

## Intellectual predecessors and objections

**Other minds / direct perception:** the [Stanford Encyclopedia of
Philosophy, "Other Minds"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/other-minds/)
documents positions that treat perceptual and second-person experience
as sources of knowledge of another mind, rather than assuming
knowledge always arises as indirect theoretical inference. The new
§1.13 deliberately rejects that false dichotomy while retaining the
fact that a perception can be mistaken. It does **not** settle which
theory of interpersonal knowledge is correct.

**Social ontology / joint action:** Michael Bratman's [*Shared Agency:
A Planning Theory of Acting Together*](https://academic.oup.com/book/11830)
(2014) and Margaret Gilbert's [*Joint Commitment: How We Make the
Social World*](https://academic.oup.com/book/9917) (2014) offer
substantial prior theories of joint activity and jointly constituted
social relations. An agreement being created by two agents is not a
novel metaphysical claim. The narrower value of §§4.32–4.33 is to
make explicit an **intelligible kind of constitutive state** rather
than treating any improved report as evidence that romantic
compatibility itself was brought into existence.

**Temporality:** dynamic relational models can have path dependence.
The earlier [Lean 4 dynamics](../../lean/MutualAcquaintanceDynamics.lean)
include a finite order-sensitivity witness. That example supplies a
logical possibility under specific transition rules, not evidence
that human relationships *always* display the same noncommutativity.

**Skeptical reply:** A person may have had a stable propensity to
make an agreement under exactly those circumstances. Does that mean
the agreement itself pre-existed? No: a conditional disposition
("would agree if asked") differs from the actual fact of agreement
("both have agreed"). This conceptual contrast is useful, but it is
well within established philosophy of dispositions and social
ontology. Its extension to reported attraction or latent
compatibility cannot be assumed without further argument.

**Methodological reply:** An observed report increase after shared
interaction could reflect selection, individual skill gains,
preferences, shifted reporting or a relational transition. The
[research iteration r0.2](../../advances/r0.2.0-draft.1/PERMISSION_RELATIVE_IDENTIFICATION.md)
proposes a way to *discipline* that inference but has not
established unique identification.

## Editorial boundaries

This version keeps:

- the single concluding §7, which remains a philosophical synthesis;
- the Indonesian source as authoritative;
- the earlier 119-proposition text and the v2.0.0-draft.1 text as
  independent historical artifacts;
- all 119 crosswalk entries, now pointing to draft.2 primary
  destinations as **conceptual** mappings, not literal equivalences;
- no new assertions of empirical validity, mathematical novelty, or
  Wittgenstein scholarship beyond the structural analogy.

The current mathematical working draft and prior Lean modules largely
refer to the earlier 119-proposition edition. A revised theorem
correspondence for draft.2 remains open; do not infer it automatically
from the crosswalk.

## What would justify v2.0.0 rather than another draft?

Independent philosophers should challenge the direct-perception
claim, the ontological distinction between dispositions and joint
facts, the consent framing, and whether each remaining proposition
actually contributes to the central argument. Literary readers
should check whether the numerical structure serves the reasoning
rather than disguises repeated commonplaces. The newest research
must survive comparison with standard statistical theory and
relationship science **without** implying that a passing Lean file
proves human behavior.

No publication, review acceptance, Git release tag, or empirical
finding is asserted.
