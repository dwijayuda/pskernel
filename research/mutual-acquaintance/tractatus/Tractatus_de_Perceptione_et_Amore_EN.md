# Tractatus de Perceptione et Amore

*On perception, attraction, and possibility.*

## 1. On someone we know

**1.** The person we know is not always the same as the person we think we know.

**1.1** We get to know other people through what we can see, hear, and experience with them. Through stories, conversations, little habits, and the way they respond to things.

**1.11** Little by little, we put together a picture of them. Sometimes, without realizing it, we add our own guesses and hopes to that picture.

**1.12** Suppose there are two people, A and B. Let P_B(A) be B's picture of A, and P_A(B) be A's picture of B. Both pictures can change over time.

**1.2** A is a real person, while P_B(A) is a picture of that person. They are not the same thing.

**1.21** That picture may be right in some respects and mistaken in others. Even after knowing someone for a long time, there may be little habits or parts of their life we have never seen.

**1.22** The more we interact, the more chances we have to know someone. But having more interactions does not always mean understanding them more accurately.

**1.3** The funny thing is, we can feel so sure that we know someone, when what we are really sure of is the picture we made of them ourselves.

**1.31** Being very sure does not make that picture any truer. We still need evidence.

**1.4** Two people can know the same person and have different pictures of them.

**1.41** That does not necessarily mean one of them is wrong. They may simply have seen different parts of the same person's life.

**1.42** Someone who hardly speaks in a group might have plenty to say when it is just the two of you. Both impressions can be true. We have only seen them in different situations.

**1.5** Feelings that grow from such a picture can still be entirely real.

**1.51** We can genuinely like someone and still misunderstand parts of who they are.

**1.52** The sincerity of a feeling and the accuracy of our knowledge are two different things. Neither automatically proves the other.

## 2. On what we know and what we only suppose

**2.** Not everything we think about someone is something we actually know.

**2.1** Some things we have seen ourselves, some we have heard directly, some we have inferred, and some we have only hoped for.

**2.11** The trouble is, all four can feel equally convincing in our heads.

**2.2** Say someone does not reply to a message. We can think of all kinds of explanations. Perhaps they are busy, perhaps they are exhausted, perhaps they forgot, or perhaps they just do not feel like talking.

**2.21** All these possibilities are worth considering. But something making sense does not make it true.

**2.22** Having no explanation does not make the explanation we like best the most likely one either.

**2.3** Sometimes a new experience makes us reconsider something that had felt certain before.

**2.31** If we use everything new merely to confirm an old assumption, perhaps it is the assumption itself that we are trying to hold on to.

**2.32** A reality that differs from our hopes is still reality.

**2.4** We often wonder what other people think of us, too.

**2.41** We do not just try to know other people. We even try to understand ourselves through guesses about how they see us.

**2.42** But a guess about somebody else's thoughts remains a guess, however long we turn it over in our minds.

**2.5** Sometimes we know what happened without understanding why it happened.

**2.51** We can watch someone do something without knowing what they were thinking when they did it.

**2.52** The reason is still in question. The event itself is something we have already seen.

**2.6** Not every question gets a complete answer.

**2.61** Seeing the same habit over and over may help us know that habit better. But how someone behaves in a different situation is still another question.

**2.62** Some questions may only be answered after something happens. Others cannot yet be answered with what is available now.

**2.7** Knowing someone well enough also depends on the choice being considered.

**2.71** What we need to know to spend an afternoon with someone may not be enough to build a life with them.

## 3. On the person we want

**3.** Everyone may have some picture of the person they would want as a partner.

**3.1** Let I_A be A's preferences in a partner, and I_B be B's preferences.

**3.11** These are not real people, but pictures of the character, values, and qualities we look for in someone.

**3.12** Such a picture need not be complete. Even the person who holds it may not quite know how to explain it.

**3.2** Sometimes we only discover what matters to us after meeting someone, going through something, or realizing that what once seemed important was not so important after all.

**3.21** What we want does not always stay the same. We gain experience, and we change. Sometimes our ideas about relationships change without our even noticing.

**3.22** There are qualities we have liked for years but only recognize when we find them in someone. There are others that only begin to matter after we experience them ourselves.

**3.23** The first helps us recognize an old desire. The second changes the desire itself. They sound similar, and sometimes even we cannot easily tell the difference.

**3.3** When B feels that P_B(A) fits I_B, B is comparing a picture of A with the qualities B wants. There may still be sides of A that B has never seen.

**3.31** That may explain why B is attracted to A. But it does not tell us how accurately B knows A, let alone how A sees B.

**3.4** There is something interesting about an ideal partner. We can name all sorts of qualities we want, yet meeting someone who has them does not guarantee that the feelings we imagined will follow.

**3.41** On the other hand, someone who does not quite fit our original picture may become deeply meaningful to us.

**3.42** Maybe people are not as simple as a checklist of traits, with boxes to tick one by one.

**3.5** Two people can have similar personalities, yet being around them can feel very different.

**3.51** We do not always like someone simply for the traits they possess. There are stories, moments, and shared experiences that make their presence different from anyone else's.

**3.6** Attraction, compatibility, and willingness to enter a relationship are three different things.

**3.61** We may be attracted to someone yet find that things do not work well when we do something together. We may also feel compatible without feeling the attraction we expected.

**3.62** Someone who has qualities we like is not necessarily someone who wants to build a relationship with us.

**3.63** If the relationship we hope for includes a willingness to choose each other, then the unwillingness of either person means that one of its conditions has not been met.

**3.64** The good qualities we see in someone do not simply disappear because one possible relationship never comes about.

## 4. On intentions, words, and interpretation

**4.** What we mean to say is not always what someone else understands.

**4.1** In conversation there is what someone intends to convey and the way another person understands it. Sometimes the two meet, sometimes they do not.

**4.11** Sometimes they fit together nicely. Sometimes they miss by a little. Sometimes we only realize later just how far apart what was meant and what was heard turned out to be.

**4.2** Something can be said honestly and still be understood in a way the speaker never intended.

**4.21** A story can make a small habit suddenly look different. The habit itself may not have changed at all.

**4.3** An action can reveal something of a person's intention. But the same action does not always have only one possible interpretation.

**4.31** Sometimes we get caught up wondering what someone else might be thinking about us. Repeating the same guess over and over does not mean we know their mind any better.

**4.4** Attention, time, and effort can be ways of showing that something matters to a person.

**4.41** But attention does not always mean romantic attraction. Neither does kindness, care, or a willingness to listen.

**4.42** Someone can put a great deal of time and energy into something that matters to them. That does not mean the person watching understands it in the same way.

**4.5** Telling each other stories gives two people a chance to learn things they did not know before.

**4.51** But knowing someone's stories by heart does not necessarily mean understanding them as that person meant them.

**4.52** How we listen matters, too. Asking again when we do not understand, responding, or realizing that we missed the point earlier. All these are part of a conversation.

**4.53** Some conversations leave us feeling understood. Whether the other person really caught what we meant is another question.

**4.6** Not everything left unsaid carries a hidden meaning.

**4.61** Silence can have many reasons. Perhaps someone is thinking, has not found the right words, or simply feels no need to say anything.

**4.62** We may read something into someone's silence. But what we take from it may not be what they are actually thinking or feeling.

**4.7** We can understand a story about an experience without ever having lived through that experience ourselves.

**4.71** Words can broaden our understanding, but they do not always make two people's experiences the same.

## 5. On two people making a choice

**5.** A relationship between two people cannot be determined by what only one of them wants.

**5.1** In game theory, the outcome of an interaction can depend on the choices of several people, each with their own goals and preferences.

**5.11** We can choose what we want to do, but we cannot always choose how another person will respond.

**5.2** Two people can hear the same story, consider the same thing, and arrive at different choices. Neither has to be wrong.

**5.21** What one person treasures may seem ordinary to another.

**5.22** Their choices may differ precisely because the same thing means something different to each of them.

**5.3** Let W_A(B,t) mean that A is willing to be in a relationship with B at time t, and W_B(A,t) mean that B is willing to be in a relationship with A at the same time.

**5.31** If H(A,B,t) means a relationship that both freely take part in at time t, then:

H(A,B,t) ⇒ W_A(B,t) ∧ W_B(A,t)

**5.32** In other words, such a relationship requires the willingness of both people.

**5.33** Even when both are willing, the relationship may not unfold as they imagined. There is time, circumstance, ability, and so much else that they may not yet have considered.

**5.4** Thinking about how a relationship might work is not the same as judging everything about the people involved.

**5.41** Knowing that someone is patient, for example, does not tell us everything about what it would be like to face a problem with them.

**5.5** Knowing what someone likes may help us understand or anticipate their choices.

**5.51** But when an outcome depends on two people, one person's choice alone is not enough to determine it.

**5.6** Two people do not need exactly the same understanding to make a choice together.

**5.61** They can remember the same meeting in different ways. They need not think alike about everything in order to choose, together, to be in a relationship.

## 6. On change and possibilities not yet experienced

**6.** People can learn, change, and better themselves.

**6.1** We may become more patient, more confident, better at speaking or listening. Sometimes we only notice the small changes when we face something that used to be difficult for us.

**6.11** Sometimes change begins with something lovely. Sometimes it begins with something that did not go as we hoped.

**6.2** Meeting someone can sometimes set off changes we never planned.

**6.21** Some people make us notice our own shortcomings. Others help us discover something that matters to us, though it had never crossed our minds before.

**6.22** Such changes do not always change the way that person sees us.

**6.3** There are things about ourselves we only learn after going through certain situations.

**6.31** We can imagine ourselves staying calm in some situation. But when it really happens, things may not turn out the way we pictured them.

**6.4** The same goes for possibilities we have not experienced.

**6.41** Sometimes we wonder about the things we never tried. What if we had made a different choice back then? What if there really was a chance we missed?

**6.42** We can imagine many answers, but we cannot live every possible life at once.

**6.43** Something that could have happened is not something that certainly would have happened. Imagining the best possible outcome does not prove that it would have been the one to occur.

**6.5** A shared experience can help two people know each other better, even if they do not learn the same things from it.

**6.51** In one event, someone might notice how the other person solved a problem. The other might remember how it felt to work through the problem together.

**6.52** Maybe their attention was different from the start. One remembers the problem, the other remembers what it felt like to face it together.

**6.6** Sometimes what grows out of a shared experience is more than knowledge about someone's traits.

**6.61** A conversation may teach us something we did not know before. Sometimes it is only after the conversation ends that we realize something in ourselves has changed, too.

**6.62** Something may also take shape during the conversation: the way two people listen and respond to one another, and whether talking together feels comfortable or uncomfortable.

**6.63** Getting to know someone, becoming aware of what we want, and experiencing a change in a relationship are three different things. Still, all three can happen in the same meeting.

**6.64** So, as two people get to know each other, they do more than observe one another. What happens between them can change them both.

**6.7** Before a meeting, we can imagine what it will feel like. But the meeting itself may not follow our imagination.

**6.71** Some things only become apparent once we have actually experienced them. Sometimes they are things we never even thought about beforehand.

**6.8** Words can explain what we know and what we only suppose. But saying a guess out loud does not turn it into reality.

**6.81** Some things only become visible when two people actually do something together. A list of traits may leave part of that experience out.

**6.82** Yet what we see does not always reveal every intention, thought, or feeling behind it.

**6.9** So there is a difference between what we know about someone, what we experience with them, and what we still do not know.

**6.91** Shared experiences may help us know someone better. But along the way, what we want and the relationship taking shape between us can change, too.

**6.92** Some things were already there; we simply did not know them yet. Others only come into being when two people experience something together.

## 7. On what is said and what is shown

**7.** We can know a great deal about someone. But as we get to know each other, some things come into view, some take shape for the first time, and some remain unknown. Not everything we experience together can be put into words.
