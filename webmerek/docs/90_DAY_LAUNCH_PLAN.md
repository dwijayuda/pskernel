# 90-day launch plan and 12-month roadmap

**T0 is the date the owner authorizes and clears operational launch, not automatically 25 October 2026.** This implementation does not authorize publication, purchases, outreach, hiring, account creation or payment acceptance. Role assignments below require actual people before promises are made.

## Days 1–14: validate before selling availability

Owner: verify legal operator, commercial name/domain/trademark, business contact and tax/invoicing arrangements. Review contract/privacy with suitable advisers [L1–L3]. Choose a commercial-compatible host, not automatic GitHub Pages production [T1]. Delivery lead: confirm hours, roles, workflow and review capacity. Research lead: recruit the 20-person interview cohort with permission.

Deliverables: evidence-backed problem notes, real availability, revised offers, verified contact, approved privacy/terms, production QA plan. Exit gate: no invented proof, all launch blockers documented and resolved or explicitly preventing launch.

## Days 15–30: first paid pilot and comprehension testing

Test the website with five target buyers for clarity, not statistical conversion claims. Offer one suitable pilot with the full stated scope and price, without fabricated discounts. Measure every delivery phase and readiness delay. Begin no more than two concurrent projects. Publish only after explicit owner approval; keep the preview accessible offline until then.

Target hypothesis: five qualified conversations and one paid project. If the first 20 carefully matched outreach attempts produce fewer than five substantive conversations, review audience/message/channel rather than scale messaging volume. Small samples are directional, not proof of market failure.

## Days 31–60: repeat the process, do not broaden the catalog

Deliver two additional pilot projects if qualified demand exists. Test consultant versus training-provider fit. Log lost-deal reasons. Obtain written permission before using any client identity or testimonial. Compare actual hours to planned 20/32/48-hour envelopes. Update pricing and scope where needed.

Create one factual case study: problem, exact work, timeline, deliverables, limitations and client-approved observations. Do not invent before/after conversion outcomes. Start a small referral-partner conversation only after successful delivery. No automatic care enrollment.

Exit gate: at least two delivered projects, acceptable handover, proposed gross margin ≥50% including founder delivery labor, no repeated unexplained >20% hour overrun. If this fails, fix operations before advertising.

## Days 61–90: choose the niche and controlled acquisition

Review the full 60-prospect/15-conversation/6-proposal/3-win planning funnel against actual counts. Choose the segment with useful customer outcomes, manageable content requirements and profitable delivery—not simply the most likes. Offer optional care only where a real update need exists.

Authorize a capped acquisition experiment only after privacy, measurement and contact work. Review cash CAC and fully loaded CAC separately. Publish one practical article per two weeks rather than low-quality volume. Limit new services until capacity is stable.

Day-90 decision: continue, narrow, change product (for example CMS assessment), or pause. Record evidence behind the decision. Three customers do not establish a national market, but can reveal whether the operating assumptions are plausible.

## Weekly operating review

Record interviews, qualified inquiries, proposals, signed scopes, cash collected, earned revenue, direct costs, actual hours, waiting days, revision causes, unresolved defects and next capacity slots. Review a 13-week cash register. Distinguish sales pipeline from bank balance. Remove unnecessary personal data according to the reviewed retention process.

## Budget governance

The financial model offers scenarios, not an approved spend. No ad spend, legal retainer, hosting purchase or contractor payment has been made. Proposed 90-day cash acquisition limits and startup allowances must fit the owner's actual reserve and signed commitments. Stop a test when its pre-agreed cap is reached; do not automatically fund losses.

## Months 4–12

| Period | Focus | Evidence required before expansion |
|---|---|---|
| Months 4–6 | Choose one strongest segment; improve briefs, content patterns and QA | Repeatable delivery and genuine permissioned case evidence |
| Months 7–9 | Partner referrals and a capped care base | Retention need, support capacity and measured contribution |
| Months 10–12 | Test a repeat-client sprint or a narrowly useful template | Repeated requests, reusable rights-cleared components and viable support burden |

Do not launch a large template store, complex application team or broad social-media agency simply because competitors offer them. The initial business must earn the right to add complexity.
