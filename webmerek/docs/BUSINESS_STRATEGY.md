# WebMerek business strategy

Decision version: 25 October 2026. **All WebMerek business decisions in this document are proposals to validate.** The evidence base and its limits are in [market research](INDONESIAN_MARKET_RESEARCH.md), [competitors](COMPETITOR_ANALYSIS.md) and [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md).

## Decision

Launch a **branding-first productized service with a premium/custom boundary**, not a general digital agency and not a cheap unlimited website subscription. The initial customer is an established owner-led consultant or professional training business with a real offer, genuine proof, an available decision maker, and a relatively simple information-site need.

Positioning: **WebMerek helps expert service businesses clarify their offer, align their identity and build a website that supports informed inquiries.** Customer-facing line: **Biar kualitasnya terlihat.** The outcome promised is a defined set of strategic/design/website deliverables—not revenue, Google ranking, fame or guaranteed leads.

## Models considered

| Model | Benefit hypothesis | Principal risk | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bespoke premium agency | High flexibility and larger possible fees | Long sales cycle, uncertain scope, no initial client proof | Selective later pathway, not default |
| Fixed branding + website offers | Easier comparison, delivery planning and reusable process | Price/scope mismatch if not measured | **Initial core** |
| Cheapest template production | Lower production time | Competes against established low monthly floors; support can erase margin | Not the primary positioning |
| Strategy-only consultancy | High intellectual differentiation | Harder to sell before trust and case evidence | Included as a bounded foundation, not a stand-alone premium claim |
| Industry-specific offers | Clearer examples and repeatable content | Premature narrowing based on no buyer interviews | Test two adjacent cohorts first |
| Large DIY/template catalog | Potential scalable distribution | Marketing, support, licensing and product maintenance before demand | Defer |

This selection is a reasoned operating choice, not a weighted “market score” masquerading as survey evidence. Flourish demonstrates an offer ladder [F1–F6]; Indonesian competitors show that both low-cost and integrated options already exist [C1–C10]. Neither establishes demand for this exact new offer.

## Ideal customer and exclusion criteria

Primary: consultant/consultancy or training provider with previous paying clients, one or a few service lines, permissioned materials, and a decision maker who can review consolidated feedback. Test a proposed project investment of Rp4.9–11.9m, not a revenue threshold guessed from appearance.

Secondary: small design/interior/professional-service studio with ready imagery and simple information needs. Accept only where delivery remains within scope.

Do not prioritize businesses still deciding what to sell, customers needing daily self-service CMS changes under the static package, complex e-commerce, health/personal-data platforms, or buyers expecting guaranteed acquisition results. Women-led businesses are welcome; no evidence supports imposing Flourish's exact gender-specific niche on Indonesia.

## Value ladder and differentiation

Start with free educational articles and a transparent local-only self-check; proceed to a short fit conversation, a paid fixed-scope project, and optional capped care. Do not gate every helpful interaction behind an email form.

The proposed difference is a combination: niche-specific explanation, strategy before visuals, realistic production limits, transparent pricing hypotheses, and client-owned handover. Each individual feature is copyable. Defensibility would have to emerge from demonstrated buyer understanding, case evidence, reliable delivery, referral relationships and a reusable content/process library—not a logo or AI tool.

## Trust before scale

Until real work exists, display clearly labeled fictional concepts, process detail and actual technical evidence. Do not borrow the repository owner's engineering achievements as agency client credentials. Do not create fake scarcity, team biographies, office addresses, testimonials or “trusted by” logos.

The site is a complete preview and useful preparation tool, but the business is not open for transactions. Operator identity, service capacity, contacts and legal requirements remain approval gates.

## Operating constraints

Two simultaneous production projects initially. Planning delivery capacity is 160 hours/month across an explicitly assembled delivery team, **not an assertion that one founder has 160 billable hours after sales/admin**. Define actual staffing before selling slots. Founder sales/management time is budgeted separately; delivery time remains a real cost even if the founder does not draw cash immediately.

Cap revisions and content, pause schedules while inputs are missing, and maintain a change-order process. Avoid long installment credit during launch. Keep care optional and retain a handover path to another provider.

## Go/no-go criteria

After three paid pilots, continue only if buyers understand the offer, delivery stays near planned hours, the gross margin including founder labor remains at least a proposed 50%, and clients can use the handover. These are management thresholds, not proven industry benchmarks. If scope overruns exceed 20% on two pilots, fix scope or prices before increasing acquisition. If most eligible buyers require a CMS, redesign the website product rather than conceal its limitations.

Do not equate likes, quiz scores or WhatsApp button clicks with customers. Track qualified inquiries, proposals, deposits, earned revenue, costs and referral consent separately.

## Twelve-month direction

Q1: validate segments and three offers with actual delivery. Q2: choose the strongest niche and publish permissioned evidence; reduce rework. Q3: build partner/referral channels and a small sustainable care base. Q4: test a template or repeat-client sprint only when repeated client needs and support capacity justify it. Reassess monthly; these are milestones, not guaranteed growth or a promise to hire.
